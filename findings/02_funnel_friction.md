# Funnel Analysis and Friction Report

## 1. Executive Summary

The funnel analysis examined 10,000 unique users across five stages, from Homepage visits to Signup Completion. Of these users, 1,301 completed signup, resulting in an overall conversion rate of 13.01%.

The analysis identifies two important areas of friction: the transition from Product View to Add to Cart accounts for the largest absolute user loss, while the transition from Add to Cart to Checkout Start has the highest percentage drop-off. Device segmentation further reveals substantially weaker mobile conversion, particularly at the checkout entry stage.

## 2. Overall Funnel Performance

The largest absolute loss occurs between Product View and Add to Cart, where 3,335 users are lost. This represents a step drop-off rate of 49.55%.

However, the highest percentage drop-off occurs between Add to Cart and Checkout Start. Of the 3,396 users who added products to their carts, only 1,633 proceeded to checkout, representing a 51.91% drop-off.

The final transition, from Checkout Start to Signup Completion, performs comparatively well, with a step conversion rate of 79.67%.

These findings distinguish the stage responsible for losing the greatest number of users from the stage with the highest proportional friction.

## 3. Desktop vs Mobile Performance

Desktop consistently outperforms mobile across the funnel.

The most significant disparity occurs between Add to Cart and Checkout Start. Desktop converts 61.14% of users at this transition, compared with only 30.14% on mobile — a difference of 31 percentage points.

Mobile experiences a 69.86% drop-off at this stage, compared with 38.86% on desktop.

The gap narrows considerably at the final transition: 80.20% of desktop users and 78.19% of mobile users who start checkout complete signup. This suggests that the primary mobile bottleneck occurs before checkout begins rather than during signup completion.

Overall homepage-to-signup conversion is 19.26% on desktop and 6.75% on mobile.

## 4. Business Implications and Recommendations

The findings suggest two investigation priorities:

1. **Product-to-cart engagement:** Investigate why a substantial number of users viewing products do not add them to their carts.
2. **Mobile checkout entry:** Prioritize a review of the mobile cart and checkout-entry experience. Navigation, layout, interaction difficulty, and unexpected checkout requirements are possible hypotheses to investigate, not causes established by this dataset.

Further analysis using device-specific session recordings, usability testing, and checkout interaction events could help identify the underlying reasons.

## 5. Conclusion

The analysis identifies mobile checkout entry as the most pronounced device-specific friction point. Improving this transition may provide an opportunity to increase mobile conversion. The results also highlight the importance of addressing product-to-cart engagement, which accounts for the largest absolute loss of users.
